No. Finding this style helpful does not mean you have ADHD.

It may simply mean you prefer clear, low-friction information—something many people do. ADHD is diagnosed from a persistent pattern of attention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity symptoms that meaningfully affect daily life, usually across settings and over time.

If you’re wondering about ADHD, notice whether difficulties with focus, organization, time, impulsivity, or task initiation are recurring and causing real problems. A qualified clinician can assess that; a communication preference alone can’t.
